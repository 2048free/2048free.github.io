2048 Hexagon

Same idea as 2048, different shape. The board is a hexagon of 19 cells, and instead of four directions you can slide tiles six ways. It sounds like a small change, but it changes how you plan almost every move.

How to play 2048 Hexagon

Each move slides every tile as far as it can go in one direction. Two tiles with the same number that collide merge into their sum, and a new 2 (sometimes a 4) appears. Build a 2048 tile to win, or keep going for a bigger score.

On a keyboard, the six directions sit on Q W E and A S D, laid out just like the arrows on the board:

↖Q
↑W
↗E
↙A
↓S
↘D

The arrow keys only cover four of them: ↑ and ↓ match W and S, while ← and → match Q and E. The two lower diagonals exist only on A and D, so if you play with a keyboard, keep your hand on the letters. On a phone, just swipe on the board and the game picks whichever of the six directions is closest to your swipe.

Why it plays differently

There's no straight left or right. Tiles move up, down, or along four diagonals, so the neat rows you'd build in classic 2048 don't exist here. In return, most cells have more neighbors: the center touches six others, and the cells along the edge touch three or four. More neighbors means more chances to merge, but also more room for a stray tile to wedge itself between two big ones.

Tips from playing it

  • Treat the bottom point of the hexagon as your corner. S, A, and D all push tiles downward, and none of them can move a tile out of that bottom cell. You can play most of a game with just those three keys and keep your biggest tile parked there.
  • Reach for W, Q, or E only when nothing below can move, and think about where the new tile might land first.
  • With only 19 cells, the board fills up fast. Merge small tiles early instead of saving them for later.
  • The game ends when every cell is full and no two neighbors match. Undo gives you one step back, which is usually enough to get out of a bad upward slide.

FAQs

Is 2048 Hexagon harder than the classic game?

Not exactly harder, just different. You get three more cells than the 4×4 board and two extra directions, but no straight rows to hide behind. Players who rely on the corner strategy usually need a few games to adjust.

Why doesn't the left arrow move tiles straight left?

Because there is no straight left on this board. The columns of hexagons run up and down, so ← slides tiles up and to the left (the same as Q), and → slides them up and to the right (the same as E).

Can I play on my phone?

Yes. Swipe on the board in any of the six directions. You don't need to be precise, since the game picks the direction closest to your swipe. Swiping outside the board scrolls the page instead.

Is my best score saved?

Yes, in your browser on this device, separately from the other games on this site. Clearing your browser data resets it.
